Understanding EMI & Loans

How EMI Works

Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment you make to a lender each month.

  • Early Years: The majority of your EMI goes towards paying the Interest.
  • Later Years: The majority goes towards reducing the Principal amount.
  • Impact of Tenure: A longer loan reduces your monthly EMI, but dramatically increases the total interest you pay.
Common Mistakes
  • Focusing only on the EMI: A car salesman might say "It's only ₹15k a month!" but hide the fact it's a 7-year loan with huge interest.
  • Not prepaying: Making even one extra EMI payment a year can cut years off a home loan.
  • Maxing out eligibility: Just because a bank approves you for a massive loan doesn't mean you should take it. Keep EMI < 40% of income.
The Mathematics Behind It

The EMI calculation uses the reducing balance method. The formula is:

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n - 1]

P = Principal Loan Amount
r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate / 12 / 100)
n = Total number of monthly installments (Years × 12)
